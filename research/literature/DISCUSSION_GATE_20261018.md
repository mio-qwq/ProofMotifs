## Decision

The broad claim that mathematics lacks a unified cancellation tool is not established. Neither the accessible discussion nor the inspected sources establish that cancellation dominates the AI corpus or is more prevalent than in human mathematics. These are separate hypotheses requiring separate evidence. In particular, declaring every equality A=B to be cancellation of A-B makes the category too broad to support informative prevalence or transfer claims.

Continue precise assumption/certificate/transfer theorems: state the objects, scalars, vanishing order, structural hypotheses and quantitative conclusion; preserve counterexamples when a transfer drops a needed hypothesis. A useful new abstraction must prove something beyond renaming kernels and zero identities, ideally through an application on a previously unexamined object. The current finite-field research is a concrete mathematical line, but does not by itself establish cross-domain unification.

For any empirical continuation, predefine the cancellation criterion, sample at the problem-family level, record selection and uncertain labels, and use mathematically matched human-written controls before making AI-specific comparisons. A targeted case collection remains appropriate for discovering mechanisms but cannot estimate their overall frequency.

## Finite-field incidence-affinity route: adversarial and literature gate

The proposed PM0013 route concerns the nonzero squares S in F_(3^m), with distinct zero-sum triples as hyperedges, and the F3-valued incidence kernel C. This reviewer accepts the following reductions, subject to the coordinator's final integrated proof and exact small-field certificates. The reported F243 rank 115/nullity 6 and separate automorphism computation were supplied by other researchers; this reviewer did not rerun them.

If dim C=m+1, C consists of affine restrictions: these already lie in C, and |S|=(q-1)/2>q/3 ensures that S affinely spans the field over F3. Pulling coordinate functions back along a hypergraph automorphism therefore extends it to an affine map L+b. Its image size forces L invertible. Since sum(S)=0 and |S|=1 in F3, summing the permutation identity gives b=0. Thus the remaining step is the classical classification of additive square preservers, after normalizing by L(1).

The proposed general kernel proof is also coherent. For u+v=w+z, the four auxiliary square values a, -u-a, -w-a and u+a-z give four zero-sum equations whose signed combination is f(u)+f(v)-f(w)-f(z)=0. Unless the original relation is tautological, the four affine forms have distinct roots. The asserted lower bound (q-38-11 sqrt(q))/16 is positive for q>=243; it comes from character expansion, the pair sums, degree-three/four Weil bounds and removal of four roots. This reviewer initially sought extra exclusions for repeated vertices in an auxiliary triple. That objection is withdrawn: in characteristic three, a repeated zero-sum triple has all three entries equal, so its equation is automatically 3f(s)=0. Crucially, f takes values in F3 because it belongs to C. No additional diagonal exclusions are needed. Freiman-2 identities then make f(a+x)-f(a) independent of the representation; triple translated-square intersections yield additivity and hence an affine extension. Small cases m=3,4 still require their separate exact certificates; finite computation alone would not prove the general step.

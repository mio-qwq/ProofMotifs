# Primary research program: extracting cancellation tools from AI proofs

Established: 18 October 2026. This program governs topic selection; the
finite-field workstreams are calibration and supporting mathematics.

## Central mathematical objective

Investigate whether repeated exact higher-order cancellations in AI-produced
proofs expose a missing reusable mathematical instrument: extract their common
structure, compare it with existing theory, and try to develop a tool that can
systematically discover, predict or control cancellations across a class of
problems. If such a tool exists, test whether it makes further problems easier
through a proved transfer or independently checked new conclusion.

This is the primary mathematical task. Capability measurement and mechanism
frequency are supporting tests of its motivating premises, not substitutes for
developing mathematics. Do not postpone mechanism research until unavailable
attempt logs or a large matched corpus can be obtained. Conversely, do not
assume that all impressive AI claims are correct, every cancellation is
higher-order, or that a tool is missing before comparing existing results.

The leading question is: **what operation could a reusable tool perform that
currently has to be discovered separately in each proof?** Possible answers
include finding a useful representation or auxiliary object, constructing
weights/symmetries/certificates, predicting the first surviving term, or
transporting a mechanism while preserving its actual hypotheses. These remain
candidates; new mathematics and better synthesis with known mathematics are
separate possible outcomes.

## Central questions

1. Which reported AI mathematical successes survive statement, proof,
   dependency and provenance checks, and what explains the observed performance?
2. Is structural cancellation a recurring, load-bearing mechanism in those
   successes? Is it unusually prevalent relative to matched mathematical work?
3. Do established mathematical tools already explain and reproduce the useful
   mechanisms, or is there a precise gap for a new cancellation-related tool?

These questions are research hypotheses. Neither general AI superiority,
cancellation predominance, a common hidden mechanism, nor the need for a new
mathematical theory is assumed. A negative answer is an informative result.

The explanatory question includes **why now**: record model/version, date,
training or inference changes when disclosed, tools, verification feedback,
search budget and stopping policy. Compare systems or versions on a fixed task
population and matched resources where possible. Final-proof descriptions alone
cannot explain the timing of capability gains.

## Five testable hypotheses

| ID | Question to test | Evidence required | Current status |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| PM-H1 | What reliable capability is present in a specified AI system and problem population? | Denominators, original statements, attempt and compute policy, provenance, verification and literature status | OPEN; published output counts alone are insufficient |
| PM-H2 | Is load-bearing structural cancellation enriched in the selected AI corpus relative to matched controls? | Frozen family-level sample, semantic coding, uncertainty, independently verified comparator provenance and matching | OPEN; targeted calibration cases do not estimate prevalence |
| PM-H3 | Does access to an explicit cancellation mechanism improve solving or verification under controlled conditions? | Fixed model/tools/budget; ordinary prompt, matched information control and mechanism intervention; independent grading | OPEN; occurrence in a final proof does not establish causal importance |
| PM-H4 | Where does the best matching existing mathematics fail to supply the desired operational conclusion? | Exact theorem-to-hypothesis comparisons, attempted reductions and explicit failures; distinguish discovery from verification | OPEN; failure to retrieve a match is not a mathematical gap proof |
| PM-H5 | Does a proposed instrument give measurable or provable additional value? | Held-out applications, equal-resource existing-tool baselines, negative cases, or a lower bound in an explicitly restricted model | OPEN; abstract renaming is insufficient |

For PM-H1, distinguish a new proof, a new theorem, rediscovery, a corrected
statement, a partial solution and a vacuous interpretation. For PM-H3, an
experiment tests usefulness of an intervention; it does not reveal all causes
of model capability or establish a model-internal mechanism.

Keep correctness conditional on publication separate from an attempt-level
solve rate. The latter requires the complete attempt denominator and stopping
policy; missing attempts do not prevent local proof audits. For PM-H2 report
both groups' component prevalences and their difference: common cancellation
without enrichment is an informative outcome. These hypotheses are not yet a
completed statistical preregistration; estimands, uncertainty, exclusions,
withdrawal handling and stopping rules must be frozen before confirmatory use.

## What counts as cancellation

Code a mechanism only when specified structure forces a nontrivial class of
contributions to vanish or yields a quantified improvement over an appropriate
non-cancellation bound. Record the scalar ring/characteristic, objects,
operations, exact hypotheses, certificate, residual term, and failure example.
Trace its role to the conclusion. Rewriting an arbitrary equality as A-B=0,
routine rearrangement, or counting occurrences of words is not sufficient.

Keep the following mechanisms distinct until a proved transfer relates them:

- polynomial/moment annihilation and finite differences;
- finite-field power sums and character orthogonality;
- analytic mean-zero or higher-moment estimates with regularity and support;
- telescoping and algorithmic summation certificates;
- sign-reversing pairings/involutions;
- chain identities and homological constructions.

Competing explanations include search and inference compute, literature
retrieval, verification and revision, representation choice, probabilistic
existence, entropy/convexity, spectral rigidity and finite computation. A
proof can use several mechanisms; code the dependency instead of assigning a
single attractive label. Mathematical cancellations can be exposed or hidden
by representation, so surface syntax is not a stable measurement.

## Prior-art and new-tool gate

Compare original statements in finite-difference/functional-degree and
augmentation-ideal theory; divided powers and characteristic-sensitive
symbols; approximation and moment/Peano-kernel theory; Fourier/character
methods; involution/homological methods; Gosper/Wilf-Zeilberger/creative
telescoping; and existing mathematical instrument extraction.

For every candidate record separately:

1. known mathematics in different notation;
2. a synthesis or certificate-discovery algorithm using known mathematics;
3. a new theorem with a precise improvement or obstruction;
4. a successful transfer to a previously unstudied target.

An instrument must specify inputs, hypotheses to check, construction or
certificate, conclusion, verification cost or quantitative gain, and failure
conditions. "Everything is in a kernel" does not meet this gate by itself.
Neither absence of a search hit nor AI success establishes historical novelty.
Necessity of a new tool is meaningful only relative to specified existing
methods and resources; unrestricted logical necessity is not the default claim.

## Evidence and sampling

Start from a versioned complete family inventory, not cancellation keyword
hits or only prominent successful claims. Treat related manuscripts as one
family. Freeze a sample and annotation rules before coding; preserve unknown
and disputed labels. Use independent annotations with adjudication and report
the actual scope of masking. Verify comparator authorship/AI involvement,
subject, theorem type and relevant proof difficulty/length before calling a
comparison matched. Inferences concern the sampled release, not all AI or all
human mathematics.

Previously inspected finite-field, DiskMaximal, PChains, MUB and withdrawn
Weil-classes examples are calibration. They cannot be reused as unseen
transfer tests. Public, abbreviated reasoning summaries are selected records,
not complete model-internal traces or a counterfactual experiment.

Verification is a separate axis: reported claim; inspected local argument;
independent exact certificate; compiled formal proof with statement/dependency
audit; specialist/human review. Formalization of a lemma does not automatically
certify the headline statement, novelty, or causal explanation.

Preserve the current
connected-normal derivation as pending supporting work rather than continuing
its degree cases as the primary agenda.

Medium term: select one concrete gap in discovering or controlling cancellation,
and inspect complete proof dependencies and alternative explanations. Develop its precise theorem,
counterexample or synthesis method, then preregister a held-out application and
baseline before attempting transfer. A literature-based causal claim requires
the actual experiment and its limitations, not the abstract alone.

Long term: determine whether a useful common tool emerges, identify its exact
scope and demonstrated cross-problem value, or show that several existing
mechanisms explain the evidence better. Report correctness, explanatory value,
novelty and formal verification independently.

Every continuation must move one of PM-H1 through PM-H5 with checkable evidence.
Specialized mathematics is prioritized when it tests or enables this program.
After two cycles without new evidence, reassess the hypothesis or direction.

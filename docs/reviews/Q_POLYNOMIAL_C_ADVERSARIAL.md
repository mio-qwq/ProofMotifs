## Verdict

ACCEPT the polynomial-seed obstruction in every characteristic: if q has
infinite multiplicative order and at least two generators have polynomial
seeds in zK[z], their K-operator representation is not free. ACCEPT the unary
exception for every nonzero polynomial seed. ACCEPT the exact quantum-plane
description for A=J_z and B=J_(z^2), after C=B+A^2.

The last notation is important: B is the operator with seed z^2, not A^2.

## 1. Independent proof of the polynomial obstruction

Let b_i=sum_(a=1..d) b_(i,a) z^a, d>=1, with zero seeds permitted. Since
q^(m+a)-1 is nonzero for every m>=0,a>=1,

    J_i(z^m)=sum_(a=1..d) b_(i,a) z^(m+a)/(q^(m+a)-1).

For a word of length L, expand over its d^L possible increment paths (zero
coefficients simply remove paths). Let the successive partial sums be
s_1<...<s_L, where 1<=s_j<=dL and L<=s_L<=dL. Put T=q^m and

    D_L(T)=product_(r=1..dL)(q^r T-1).

Each path denominator is the product of its L distinct factors indexed by
s_1,...,s_L. Therefore multiplying every word's band coefficient by D_L leaves
a polynomial in T of degree at most dL-L=(d-1)L. There are (d-1)L+1 possible
output shifts and the same number of polynomial coefficients per band. The
linear map from length-L free words to these cleared band arrays consequently
has codomain dimension at most ((d-1)L+1)^2 over K.

For n>=2, choose L with n^L>((d-1)L+1)^2. Its kernel contains a nonzero
homogeneous free polynomial. The resulting rational band identities vanish
identically in T, not just at sampled monomials. Evaluating at T=q^m is legal
for every m>=0, so the operator kills every monomial z^m.

This implies the operator is zero on all K[[z]], not only on K[z]: each J_i
raises valuation by at least one, and the coefficient of z^N in a length-L
word applied to a formal series depends only on the finitely many input
coefficients of indices <=N-L. Coefficientwise finite summation therefore
extends the monomial identity. No integer factorial or characteristic-zero
argument is involved.

An operational improvement in the root code is valid: one need not enumerate
all n^L words. Any first ((d-1)L+1)^2+1 distinct words already force dependence.

For n=1 and nonzero b, put v=ord_0(b)>=1 and let c be its leading coefficient.
Inductively J_b^k(1) has order kv and leading coefficient

    c^k/product_(r=1..k)(q^(rv)-1).

Thus the powers of J_b are K-linearly independent after evaluation on 1. A
single nonzero polynomial seed gives a faithful representation of K[x]. The
zero seed does not. This is the sharp alphabet-size boundary asserted here.

## 2. Exact cubic and the stronger mixed relation

For A=J_z, B=J_(z^2), the three words A^2B, ABA, BA^2 all shift by four.
With common denominator product_(r=1..4)(q^r T-1), their cleared numerators
are respectively qT-1, q^2T-1, q^3T-1. Therefore the raw rational identity is

    q(qT-1)-(q+1)(q^2T-1)+(q^3T-1)=0,

which proves qA^2B-(q+1)ABA+BA^2=0 as an operator on all formal series.

The stronger mixed-degree identity is

    BA-qAB=(q-1)A^3.

After clearing the three factors of D_3(T), its numerator is
(q^2T-1)-q(qT-1)-(q-1)=0. If R denotes the left side minus the right side,
then RA-AR is exactly the homogeneous cubic above.

## 3. Full quantum-plane identification

Set C=B+A^2 and now abbreviate D_s(T)=product_(r=1..s)(q^rT-1), D_0=1.
Direct addition gives

    C(z^m)=qT z^(m+2)/D_2(T),   T=q^m.

The mixed relation says CA=qAC. Applying j copies of C in sequence contributes
qT, q^3T,...,q^(2j-1)T in the numerator. Applying i subsequent copies of A
fills the remaining consecutive denominator factors. Hence, for all i,j,m>=0,

    A^i C^j(z^m)=q^(j^2) T^j z^(m+i+2j)/D_(i+2j)(T).

This formula has the correct rightmost-first orientation. For a finite
K-linear relation between these operators, group its coefficients by total
shift s=i+2j. At each shift the numerator is

    sum_(0<=j<=s/2) c_(s-2j,j) q^(j^2) T^j.

It vanishes at T=q^m for every m>=0. Infinite order gives infinitely many
distinct points, so this polynomial is identically zero. Its distinct powers
of T force every coefficient to vanish, in every characteristic.

The quotient K<X,Y>/(YX-qXY) maps onto the operator algebra generated by A,C.
Its normal monomials X^iY^j span by commuting Y past X. Their operator images
are independent by the preceding argument, so this map is an isomorphism.
Since B=C-A^2, the algebras generated by A,B and by A,C coincide. Equivalently,
the A,B algebra has the single presentation

    K<A,B>/(BA-qAB-(q-1)A^3).

This is an exact identification with an established algebraic object, not
evidence that a new quantum-algebra structure has been discovered.

## 4. Preventing an overclaim about down-up relations

The cubic has the shape of ONE defining down-up relation, with u=A,d=B,
alpha=q+1,beta=-q,gamma=0. However, the companion relation fails:

    (B^2A-3BAB+2AB^2)(1)=(2/1395)z^5,   at q=2.

It is therefore incorrect to identify the A,B presentation directly as a
down-up algebra using those parameters, or to infer both quantum-Serre
relations from the displayed cubic. The correct exact identification is the
quantum plane after replacing B by C=B+A^2.

A narrow primary-source comparison was made, not a broad literature search:
Benkart and Roby, *Down-Up Algebras*, arXiv:math/9803159v1, section 1,
PDF pages 3-4, explicitly define the algebra using both relations R1 and R2:
https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9803159 . The comparison supports the boundary
just stated, not a priority conclusion for this operator realization.

old denominator and hence divides the old numerator, or is supplied by the
new denominator's extra factors. Thus the algorithm's exact division does
not silently assume a path cancellation that can fail.

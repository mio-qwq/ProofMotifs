# PM-CYCLE10-A: independent review of the degree-13 orbit explanation

Date: 18 October 2026. The coordinator proposed this structural explanation;
this note independently verifies it. No repository files changed.

## General free-action lemma

Let ell be an odd prime different from 3, K=F_(3^ell), and F(y)=y^3.
Consider the 6ell maps

g_(eps,j,c)(y)=eps*F^j(y)+c,
eps in {1,-1}, j in Z/ell, c in F_3.

They form a group: F fixes c in F_3, so composition stays in the displayed
family. These maps are distinct. For example, equality of two maps first
gives equal constants by evaluating at zero; evaluating at 1 determines
eps; equality of Frobenius powers on K determines j modulo ell.

This group acts freely on K\F_3.

For eps=1, j=0, a fixed point requires c=0, the identity map. If j!=0
and g(y)=y, iterating ell times gives y+ell*c=y. Since 3 does not divide
ell, c=0. Then F^j(y)=y, whose fixed field is F_3 because ell is prime
and j is nonzero modulo ell.

For eps=-1, squaring g gives g^2(y)=F^(2j)(y): the two translations
cancel since F^j(c)=c. If j!=0, a fixed point therefore lies in the
fixed field of F^(2j), which is F_3 because ell is odd prime. If j=0,
the fixed-point equation 2y=c likewise puts y in F_3. Thus no nonidentity
map has a fixed point outside F_3.

Consequently, every orbit on K\F_3 has size 6ell.

## Consequence for a stable degree-13 root space

Let ell=13 and let U be a four-dimensional F_3-linear subspace of K
containing F_3 and stable under F. Then it is stable under every map g.
The invariant subset U\F_3 has 3^4-3=78=6*13 elements. Since the action
is free, U\F_3 is a single orbit.

This argument requires neither an irreducible decomposition of U nor
enumeration of its 81 elements. It requires an independently established
kernel dimension four, containing F_3 and Frobenius stable. A kernel of
L(F) with coefficients in F_3 and L(1)=0 has the last two properties.

## Square-filter invariance

Let chi be the quadratic character on K. For y outside F_3, the three
elements y-1,y,y+1 are all nonzero. Define the property

Q(y): chi(y-1)=chi(y)=chi(y+1).

Q is invariant under the three generators:

* Translating y by any c in F_3 permutes the three elements.
* Negation replaces the three elements by their negatives in a different
  order, multiplying all three characters by the same chi(-1).
* Frobenius sends each character value to its cube, equal to itself.

Thus if one y0 in U\F_3 has Q(y0), all 78 elements have Q. This proves
the complete-filter phenomenon from a single checked element and the
dimension calculation. If none is known to pass, transitivity alone only
proves that either all or none pass; it does not decide which alternative.

The filter is precisely the seed square condition for
a=1+1/y=(y+1)/y and b=-1-a=(y-1)/y: both are squares if and only if
the three displayed characters agree. Excluding y in F_3 ensures
a,b are nonzero and distinct from each other and from 1.

## Verification of the proposed irreducible-module alternative

The alternative argument with C(T)=T^3+T^2+T+2 is also sound.
C has no root in F_3, hence is irreducible. Reduction modulo C gives
T^13=1; since C(1)!=0, the class of T has exact order 13.

If U has annihilator D=(T-1)C and dimension four, squarefreeness of
T^13-1 gives U=ker(F-1) direct-sum ker C(F)=F_3 direct-sum W,
with dim W=3. For every w!=0 in W its annihilator is a nonconstant
divisor of the irreducible C, hence is exactly C. Therefore F has order
13 on w and its orbit has 13 elements. The negative of w cannot lie
in the same orbit, since F^j(w)=-w would imply w=-w after taking the
13th iterate. The group generated by F and negation acts freely with
26-element orbits on W\{0}, which itself has size 26, so it is transitive.
Translations in F_3 supply the remaining factor three on U\F_3.

The free-action proof above is shorter and has fewer dependencies.

## Limits

This explanation is specific to the stated root space and a verified
passing element. It does not prove a good seed exists, does not determine
the exact sparse incidence kernel dimension, and does not imply that
every small Frobenius-stable root space passes the square filter.
All group, field and character arguments are classical; novelty is not
claimed. No human or proof-assistant verification is represented here.

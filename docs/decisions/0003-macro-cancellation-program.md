# Decision 0003: prioritize the explanatory and transfer questions

Date: 18 October 2026.

The accumulated finite-field work gives exact structural statements and
counterexamples, but does not establish why AI succeeds at mathematics,
cancellation prevalence, or the need for a new mathematical instrument.
Continuing adjacent orbit cases alone would not answer those questions.

The controlling program therefore separates five hypotheses about capability,
prevalence, causal utility, existing-tool gaps and added instrument value.
Its first outputs are a primary-source audit, semantic codebook, frozen sample
and source-specific tool comparison. A proposed unification must supply a
checkable operation and useful conclusion, then survive a held-out transfer
test with baselines and negative cases.

The finite-field theorems remain valid at their recorded scope. The pending
connected-normal derivation is preserved for future acceptance, rather than
discarded or silently promoted to an accepted result. Specialized follow-ups
must explain their contribution to the primary research questions.

# Decision 0002: prioritize a transformation theorem over another expansion

Date: 2026-10-18.

After the SC-003 rank-only counterexample, two neighboring tasks were considered: expand four first differences (SC-004), or characterize which coordinate changes preserve all ternary cancellation orders (SC-005).

SC-004 is retained with its unverified formulas, but not promoted to a theorem. Its expected immediate output is another coefficient table, with substantial classical overlap. SC-005 asks a necessary-and-sufficient transfer question directly exposed by the counterexample. It yielded a written classification, an essential F_9 exception and exact finite preserver-group checks. This is why it was prioritized; the change was not a random abandonment of a difficult topic.

The SC-005 proof uses classical polarization, a conic parameterization, linearized polynomials and the root bound. Novelty remains unverified. Before adding more polynomial cases, prioritize an independent/semantic audit where tools permit and a source-specific comparison with finite-field preserver theorems. A useful next constructive question is how to extract a rejecting triple from a non-scalar-Frobenius additive map without testing all triples, while comparing the cost to standard linearized-polynomial tests.

No participant other than the coordinator contributed an independently executed proof. Sequential algebraic, adversarial and literature passes are recorded in the review, not counted as a research team.
